Baby-Strollers as a Service

TL;DR

Baby-Strollers as a Service is a sustainable design concept developed in a 2016 course, exploring Bugaboo as a service provider rather than solely a product retailer. Flying Solutions offers destination-based stroller rentals for traveling parents; Pop-up Solutions offers monthly rentals that adapt to a child’s growing needs, with Basic and Premium plans. The concept aims to reduce ownership and travel burdens while supporting product return and circular use; commercial feasibility and environmental benefits remain unvalidated.

Goals

Business Goals





Explore an access-over-ownership business model for Bugaboo, the brand selected in the original concept; participation or endorsement is not established.



Develop short-term travel and medium- to long-term rental offerings.



Support a circular service model in which products return to the manufacturer after use.



Validate commercial viability and environmental performance before committing to a launch by running: 1) a willingness-to-pay survey (n≥200 travelers/parents), 2) a pilot in 2 destinations with 50 rentals each, and 3) a lifecycle assessment scoped to representative routes and cleaning processes.

User Goals





Access a stroller at a travel destination without transporting one over long distances.



Rent a stroller suited to the child’s changing needs rather than purchasing equipment upfront.



Obtain relevant accessories when needed.



Return equipment when finished instead of managing its storage or disposal.

Source context: The supplied 2016 concept identifies heavy travel equipment, household storage, uncertainty about disposal, and upfront accessory purchases as pain points. It cites a stroller purchase range of €100–€400 and describes infants outgrowing equipment within months; these are historical concept assertions, not independently verified market or developmental findings. Research methods, sample sizes, and supporting sources are Unknown.

Non-Goals





Proposed: Expanding into unrelated parenting equipment in the initial service scope.



Proposed: Treating this course concept as an approved Bugaboo initiative or production launch.



Proposed: Claiming that rental inherently reduces environmental impact without lifecycle analysis.

User Stories

Traveling parent:





As a traveling parent, I want to reserve a stroller near my destination, so that I do not need to transport one on my trip.



As a traveling parent, I want to select destination-appropriate accessories, so that the stroller suits local conditions.



As a traveling parent, I want to organize rental logistics through a mobile application, so that I can plan equipment access alongside my journey.

Parent seeking everyday access:





As a parent, I want to rent a stroller monthly, so that I can access equipment without buying it outright.



As a parent, I want equipment that adapts to my child’s growing needs, so that the service remains relevant over time.



As a parent, I want to choose between essential equipment and accessories on demand, so that I can select the service level I need.



As a parent, I want to return the stroller when finished, so that I do not need to retain or dispose of it myself.

Functional Requirements





Flying Solutions (Proposed priority: Core)





Destination access: Provide a short-term stroller rental sent to the nearest destination for parents traveling with infants.



Mobile reservation: Allow parents to reserve equipment and organize logistics through a mobile application.



Modular accessories: Support accessory selection based on destination needs, including the concept’s mosquito-net and rain-umbrella examples.



Proposed: Confirm serviceable destinations, dates, equipment availability, and handoff arrangements before accepting a reservation.



Pop-up Solutions (Priority: Core)





Monthly rental: Rent rather than sell strollers for day-to-day, medium- to long-term needs.



Basic plan: Provide the stroller for a monthly fee (suggested €30–€60/mo), with return when the parent is finished.



Premium plan: Provide the stroller and additional accessories on demand for a higher monthly fee (suggested €60–€100/mo).



Growth adaptation: Support rentals based on the child’s growing needs; define equipment exchange policy allowing 1 free upgrade per 6 months and exchange fee thereafter.



Action: Implement a documented request process and SLA for equipment changes (target <7 days for exchanges) to operationalize growth adaptation.



Product return and circular use (Proposed priority: Core)





Return pathway: Return products to the manufacturer when use ends, consistent with the concept’s circular model.



Proposed: Track each asset through rental, return, inspection, cleaning, repair, and any subsequent rental or retirement.



Proposed: Release returned equipment for further rental only after defined safety and hygiene checks.

Prices, rental durations for Flying Solutions, geographic coverage, accessory availability, cancellation policies, deposits, and responsibility for loss or damage are TBD.

User Experience

Entry Point & First-Time User Experience





The concept specifies a mobile application for Flying Solutions reservations and logistics.



Proposed: Introduce the two services with a clear distinction between travel rental and monthly rental.



Proposed: Display plan inclusions, service coverage, pricing, and return obligations before booking. Acquisition channels and onboarding requirements are TBD.

Core Experience





Proposed flow — Flying Solutions:





Enter the destination and travel dates.



Review available strollers and select relevant modular accessories.



Review the total price and destination handoff arrangements, then reserve.



Receive the stroller near the destination and use it during the rental period.



Return the equipment through an agreed handoff process.



Proposed flow — Pop-up Solutions:





Select Basic or Premium and a stroller appropriate to the child’s needs.



Review monthly charges and rental terms, then begin the rental.



Use the stroller; Premium customers request accessories as needed.



Request equipment changes when the child’s needs evolve.



Return the stroller when finished.

Proposed: Define alternatives for unavailable equipment, delayed handoffs, changed travel dates, damaged equipment, and overdue returns before a pilot.



Proposed: Validate accessory compatibility and applicable child-use limits before confirming equipment selections.



Proposed: Define subscription cancellation, billing adjustments, and equipment-exchange terms explicitly.

Narrative

Proposed illustrative scenario, not observed research:

A parent is planning a trip with an infant. A stroller will be useful at the destination, but taking it along adds another large item to the journey. Rather than arranging to transport the family’s stroller, the parent opens Flying Solutions and checks whether the destination is served.

The parent selects travel dates, reviews available equipment, and adds a mosquito net appropriate to the destination. Before confirming, the service presents the rental price and the proposed handoff and return arrangements. On arrival, the parent receives the reserved stroller nearby. At the end of the trip, the equipment returns through the service rather than traveling home with the family.

For everyday use, the same family considers Pop-up Solutions. Basic covers the stroller itself; Premium includes access to additional accessories on demand. The parent chooses a monthly plan based on current needs and later requests a change as the child grows. When the equipment is no longer needed, it is returned instead of stored at home or discarded.

This scenario illustrates the intended shift from a one-time retail transaction to an ongoing service relationship. For parents, the intended outcome is access to suitable equipment without all the obligations of ownership. For the provider, the proposed opportunity is recurring service revenue and continued stewardship of physical assets. Neither outcome is established by the concept alone: reliable logistics, safe equipment reuse, pricing, and lifecycle performance would need validation before this experience could be offered responsibly.

Proposed: Contribution margin per rental or subscription period, measured from fees less attributable fulfillment, servicing, and asset costs.



Proposed: Asset utilization, measured as rented asset-days divided by serviceable asset-days using inventory records.

Technical Metrics





Proposed: Successful booking transaction rate, measured from reservation attempts and confirmed transaction records.



Proposed: Equipment readiness compliance, measured as dispatched rentals with completed required inspection and hygiene records divided by dispatched rentals.

Tracking Plan





Proposed: Track availability searches, booking starts, reservation confirmations, accessory requests, handoffs, exchanges, and returns.



Proposed: Link rental records to asset histories, operational costs, and servicing records.



Proposed: Conduct a lifecycle assessment covering production, transport, cleaning, repair, reuse, and end-of-life treatment to test environmental claims. Product return alone does not establish landfill avoidance or lower emissions.

Phase 1 — Concept validation (duration: 6–8 weeks)





Deliverables: 1) 20–30 parent interviews across traveler and local segments; 2) quantified service-demand assessment from a 200-person survey; 3) pricing hypotheses and sensitivity scenarios; 4) end-to-end operational mapping with candidate fulfillment partners; 5) initial lifecycle assessment scope and data requirements.



Responsible party: 2–3 person discovery team with named owners for research and ops engagement.



Dependencies: access to partner fulfillment teams and sample inventory for pilot planning.



Proposed phase 2 — Limited service pilot (duration: TBD)





Proposed deliverables: Narrow service coverage, a minimal reservation experience, documented handoff and return procedures, and equipment-readiness criteria.
